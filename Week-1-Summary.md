# Week 1 — Market Research & Product Discovery

## Executive summary
India's EV ecosystem is increasingly software-defined. Connected vehicles combine BMS, vehicle controls, telematics, mobile applications, cloud analytics and OTA updates. Public materials from Ather, Ola Electric and Hyundai demonstrate that software already plays a major role in charging and battery-management experiences.

The opportunity investigated here is not basic thermal monitoring. The product hypothesis is a **customer-facing orchestration layer** that connects charging-state decisions with dynamic ETA, transparent explanations, telemetry and controlled OTA rollout.

## Research questions
1. What charging problems matter most to EV users?
2. How important is charging predictability?
3. How do leading OEMs manage charging and thermal conditions?
4. Which capabilities are already table stakes?
5. Where is a defensible customer-experience white space?
6. What evidence is needed before production?

## Customer pain hypotheses
- Charging takes longer than expected.
- Charging speed changes without explanation.
- ETA is not trusted.
- Users worry about battery health.
- Technical alerts create confusion.
- Support teams lack sufficient charging-event context.

## Competitive benchmark
### Ather
Public materials describe AtherStack as an integrated software ecosystem spanning vehicles, charging infrastructure, accessories and mobile applications. Ather has also publicly documented Optimised Charging through OTA.

### Ola Electric
Public company materials describe BMS capabilities including charging control, SoC estimation, battery health and thermal management, plus telematics analytics.

### Hyundai
Hyundai documents battery conditioning that can prepare battery temperature for DC charging and can use navigation context to prepare for a charging stop in applicable vehicles.

## White-space hypothesis
The strongest opportunity is not claiming to invent thermal management. It is to make adaptive charging:
1. understandable,
2. predictable,
3. measurable,
4. progressively deployable.

## Framework outputs
### SWOT
**Strengths:** software-led, OTA-enabled, connects engineering and UX.  
**Weaknesses:** vehicle-specific integration, safety validation, telemetry dependency.  
**Opportunities:** EV growth, connected-vehicle differentiation, lower support burden.  
**Threats:** OEM proprietary systems, safety/cybersecurity risk, feature imitation.

### PESTEL
Political: EV adoption policy.  
Economic: battery, energy, warranty and service economics.  
Social: trust, safety and convenience.  
Technology: BMS, telematics, OTA, cloud.  
Environmental: battery longevity and efficiency.  
Legal: safety, cybersecurity, data privacy and vehicle software requirements.

### Porter
Rivalry: High. Buyer power: High. Supplier power: Medium–High. Substitutes: High. New entrants: Medium.
